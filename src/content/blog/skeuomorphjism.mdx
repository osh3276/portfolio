There once was a time where internet looked real. Where the camera icon had a glass lens, and the Windows icon looked like a summer breeze. Now, the camera icon is usually just a collection of same-coloured shapes, and the Window has never been more boring. God is dead, and we killed him with flat design.

Skeuomorphism in UX/UI is often associated with dated technology. The 2000s and early 2010s were rife with detailed icons and glossy [blobjects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blobject). Think the [iMac G3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMac_G3), or [Clippy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_Assistant). I fear there is this idea that skeuomorphic designs cannot look modern and fresh, which is simply untrue. Just look at this [Figma UI pack!](https://www.figma.com/community/file/1526252667314622293/skeuomorphic-ui-pack) The beautiful leather stitching and texture combined with a luxury-esque bottom-up lighting on the glass buttons certainly exudes a sense of progress and modernity. 

Despite this, softwares that include skeuomorphic design are still uncommon. This is why I was so excited when Apple introduced their Liquid Glass design language. Apple's design choices often influence the industry as a whole (iOS 7 causing a massive shift toward flat design), and Liquid Glass was certainly a move away from flat design. While it has its drawbacks, I appreciate its refreshing take on UI design. 

I think there's still a fair case to be made for flat and minimalist designs. They're very readable and clean, and can still be very elegant. [Yifan's portfolio](https://www.refact0r.dev) is what I would consider flat design, and it's quite a treat for the eyes—but too often I will find flat designs that have absolutely no personality. Perhaps this is a sign to redesign my own website...

Anyways, I've put off finishing this post for so long that MacOS 27 has been released (I started writing this like a whole month ago), and with it came Aqua-like traffic light buttons. I'd just like to say I'm a huge fan. Thank you to the Apple design chief. See you all in the next post.
