---
title: i became a bicycle mechanic (sorta)
date: "2026-06-22"
---

The title isn't entirely true, but I definitely know a lot more about repairing and maintaining bikes than I did a month ago.

Back in May when I first moved to London (Ontario) for my current summer research position, I felt that I would need a bike for running errands. My bike at home was in serious disrepair, so I had to get one in London. I never really cared much about getting good bikes, I just went to Walmart and picked out a random bike. However, both of my brakes ended up failing in the span of 3 rides. I returned the bike.

I now found myself, once again, bikeless. I didn't want to buy one from Walmart again, but the higher quality bikes at Sportchek and Canadian Tire were far too expensive for me (400+ CAD!). I decided to get a bike off of Facebook Marketplace, the holy grail of online thrifting. Seriously, there are some insane deals on there.

It was here where I first laid my eyes on a vintage road bike. Thin chromoly steel tubes gleaming in the sun and a sexy red finish: a 125 dollar Nishiki road bike. Unfortunately, it had been sold by the time I messaged the seller. I instead settled for a 150 dollar step-through model of a mid-70s Raleigh Record. I had to get the order in before my dad pulled up to help me move out (I got kicked out so I had to find a different place, long story), so I didn't end up looking through any more options.

I was initially quite happy with my purchase. However, the more I scrolled marketplace, the more unsatisfied I felt. I definitely liked the look of a diamond frame better, and there were bikes out there that sold for cheaper. So two weeks later in mid-June, I bussed halfway across the city to pick up a 1972 Raleigh Grand Prix for 80 bucks. The plan was to swap out the frame and any components I wanted from either bikes and make a nice, personal bike. I'd sell the other one. I biked the thing 10 kilometers back home and put it next to my other Raleigh. I quickly realized that the GP would probably end up being something of a project bike. I had no idea how hard it would be to maintain my own project bike, but I knew I wanted to do it. Armed with a wrench, a hammer, pliers, and a screwdriver, I went to work.

The seller gave me free handlebar tape, so one of the first things I did was rewrap the handlebars. I didn't like the original tape. There was also a lot of rust on a few of the components, so I disassembled them and dropped them into a citric acid bath (which worked surprisingly well). 

The biggest issue I ran into was with the crankset. One of the screws attaching the smaller chain ring had come loose, and the chain tension ended up significantly warping the ring. I had to take off the crank arms to take the ring out. The crank arms were held in by cotter pins (like railroad spikes), which I had to hammer out. In my inexperience, I completely destroyed the threads on one of the cotter pins, which made it impossible to attach the crank arm back to the bottom bracket properly. I ended up having to replace the cotter pins. Anyways, I ended up just removing the ring and turning my bike into a 1x5 (from a 2x5). Not a huge difference, but I want to go bikepacking someday so I'll have to replace the crankset eventually.

I also replaced the brake levers with new Tektro RL340s. The original brake levers were "suicide" levers, their poor reliability earning them the unfortunate moniker. Some people like them, though. I put them up on Facebook Marketplace for 15 bucks.  

Most of the other maintenance work I've done is really just re-lubing the chain and regreasing a few old cogs. I have a lot planned for the future, though. I want to repaint the entire frame light blue, change out the saddle for a nice leather one, and swap the handlebar tape for brown (which is being shipped as I write this). I also want to convert my 27" wheels to 700c, but I've heard from the depths of Reddit that it's more of a pain than one would expect. 27" tires are kind of rare, though.

This adventure has actually made me want to fix up the bikes back home. I definitely have a new appreciation for old bikes now, and it's pretty amazing how much life you can breathe into one with a little elbow grease. I'll probably end up posting more of my bike projects on this blog, so come back in a while if you want to see more of this.
