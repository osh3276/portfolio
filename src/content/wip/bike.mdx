---
title: i became a bike mechanic
date: "2026-06-22"
---

The title isn't entirely true, but I definitely know a lot more about repairing and maintaining bikes than I did a month ago.

Back in May when I first moved to London (Ontario) for my current summer research position, I felt that I would need a bike for running errands. My bike at home was in serious disrepair (I pretty much rode that thing into the ground for 6 years and never serviced it once), so I had to get one in London. I never really cared much about getting good bikes or any specs in general, I just went to Walmart and picked out a random bike. What I wasn't expecting was for both of my brakes to fail in the span of 3 rides. I ended up returning the bike at the end of the month (didn't end up using it much since it was cold anyways).

I now found myself, once again, without a bike. I didn't want to buy a bike from Walmart again, but the bikes at Sportchek and Canadian Tire were far too expensive for me (400+ CAD!). It was then when I remembered that I could get a bike off of Facebook Marketplace, the holy grail of online thrifting. Seriously, there are some insane deals on there.

It was on Facebook Marketplace where I first laid my eyes on a vintage steel road bike. Wiry steel, gleaming in the sun; a 125 dollar Nishiki road bike. Unfortunately, it had been sold by the time I messaged the seller. I instead settled for a 150 dollar step-through model of a mid-70s Raleigh Record. I had to get the order in before my dad pulled up to help me move out (I got kicked out so I had to find a different place, long story), so I made the order in haste.

I was initially quite happy with my purchase. But, the more I scrolled marketplace, the more I realized that I was searching for more. I definitely liked the look of a diamond frame better, and there were bikes out there that sold for cheaper. So two weeks later in mid-June, I bussed halfway across the city to pick up a 1972 Raleigh Grand Prix for 80 bucks. The plan was to swap out the frame and any components I wanted from either bikes and make a nice, personal bike. I biked the thing 10 kilometers back home and put it next to my other Raleigh. I decided then and there that the Grand Prix would become my project bike for years to come. I really did not know how hard the work of making my own project bike would actually be, but I knew I wanted to do it. Armed with a wrench, a hammer, a plier, and a screwdriver, I went to work.
